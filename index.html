Does any of this sound familiar?

These are the six things we hear most often. If two or more ring true, there is almost certainly a quick win available.

The same information gets typed in twice

An order arrives in one system and someone re-types it into another. Every re-type is a chance to get it wrong.

Month end runs on spreadsheets

Two days of exports and copy and paste that only one person in the building really understands.

Reports contradict each other

Sales, finance and production quote three different figures for the same month, and nobody can say which is right.

Everything depends on one person

The reports work because a particular colleague keeps them working. When they are on holiday, reporting stops.

Old reports nobody dares touch

Built years ago by someone who has since left. Still running, and nobody can safely change them.

Simple questions take days

Asking which products actually make money means someone has to go away and build something first.

None of this is fixed by buying more software. It is fixed by joining up what you already own, agreeing once what each number means, and letting the reports build themselves.

Questions we get asked first

If yours is not here, ask it on the call. We would rather answer up front than halfway through a project.

Do we need to replace our ERP system?

No. We work with the systems you already have. Replacing an ERP is expensive and disruptive, and in most cases the system is not the problem. The problem is that nothing joins your systems together.

We are a small manufacturer. Is this only for large companies?

No. Smaller manufacturers often feel this more sharply, because reporting usually rests on one person and their spreadsheets. Work is scoped to the size of your business, so you are not paying for something built for a company ten times your size.

How long before we see results?

You get a written summary of where your numbers disagree within about two weeks of the first call. First working reports usually follow within four to eight weeks, depending on how many systems are involved.

How much does it cost?

The first call and the written summary are free. Everything after that is quoted as a fixed price against an agreed scope, so you know the number before the work starts rather than getting an open ended hourly bill. See how pricing works.

Who owns the reports once they are built?

You do. The reports, the underlying work and the documentation are yours. Everything is written to be handed over, so your own team or another supplier can maintain it without us.

Our data is a mess. Is it too early to talk?

That is usually the reason to talk rather than a reason to wait. Nobody has tidy data before this kind of work starts. Sorting out the mess is the work.
