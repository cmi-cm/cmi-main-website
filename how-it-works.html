What We Do / How It Works

Four stages, and you can stop after any of them

Every stage produces something you keep, whether or not you continue. The initial call is free, and we offer one free data audit each month.

Start with the free call Practical questions

No pitch, no obligation.

  1. 1
    Always free

    The discovery call

    Thirty minutes on a video call. You describe which figures get argued about and what that costs you in time or money. We ask enough questions to work out whether this is something we can help with.

    Sometimes the answer isn't a data project at all, but a conversation between two departments. If that's the case, we'll tell you rather than recommend work you don't need.

    How long
    30 minutes
    Your team
    Whoever knows the problem
    You keep
    A written summary of the problem and suggested next steps
  2. 2
    One free each month

    The data audit

    We look at which system holds what, where the figures diverge, and why. You get a written report of your data, with the disagreements and their likely causes documented.

    An argument that has been running for two years might turn out to be two departments counting a credit note differently. Once the difference is documented, everyone can work from the same definition.

    We do one of these free every month. Each audit takes up to three weeks to complete, so we limit the number we take on to make sure each gets the attention it needs. If this month's slot is taken, we'll let you know when the next one is available or provide a fixed quote if you'd prefer to get started sooner.

    How long
    Up to three weeks from access
    Your team
    Roughly half a day
    You keep
    The written audit and, if you want to proceed, a fixed quote
  3. 3

    The build

    We build the data warehouse, pipelines and reports in agreed stages, with regular progress updates rather than one long silence followed by a launch. Each stage delivers something you can open and use.

    Before new reports go live, we reconcile them against the figures you already use. Where numbers differ, we trace the gap back to the underlying records and work with your team to establish which figures the business should use. You can see where every number comes from, rather than simply being asked to trust a new report.

    How long
    First reports within eight weeks of access, for an agreed scope
    Your team
    About an hour a week reviewing
    You keep
    Working reports at every stage
  4. 4

    Handover

    Documented definitions for the figures that matter, reporting access appropriate to each role, and clear ownership of the key metrics for your team to confirm.

    How long
    A few days
    Your team
    A handover session and time for questions
    You keep
    The documentation, the solution and the freedom to choose your next steps

What this asks of your team

Data projects can take up more of your team's time than expected, particularly when questions and decisions keep coming up throughout the build. The audit helps us understand your systems and the business behind the numbers before development begins.

After that, allow roughly an hour a week from one person who knows the numbers, depending on complexity. Most of that time is spent reviewing what we have built and flagging anything that doesn't look right.

That review matters. When two systems disagree, we can trace the difference and explain where it comes from, but your team needs to help establish which figure reflects how the business operates. We document the agreed definition so everyone can work from the same understanding.

You don't need to appoint a project manager or organise a steering group. We keep the process focused and involve the people who can answer the questions that matter.

What we will need

  • Read access to the relevant data sources
  • Someone we can contact when questions arise
  • A person who can help agree on business definitions
  • Copies of existing reports to check our results against

We work with your IT team to agree on the minimum permissions needed. We don't modify your existing systems or install software on production equipment.

When we tell you not to bother

Three situations where we will tell you the work is not worth doing. It's better to find that out before you commit to a project.

Data is not your problem

Sometimes two departments report different figures because they are measuring different things and nobody has agreed on the calculations that drive them. That might need a half-hour conversation, not a data project.

The savings don't justify the cost

If a reporting problem costs you two hours a month, a rebuild may never pay for itself. We would rather recommend a simpler fix than sell you something that costs more than it saves.

You are mid-change already

If you are replacing your ERP next quarter, building around the old system may be wasted effort. We will tell you if it makes more sense to wait until the new system is in place.

Practical questions about the process

The ones that come up once people have decided the idea is sound and want to know what it actually involves.

How much of our team's time will this take?

For a typical project, allow around half a day from someone who knows your systems during the audit, then roughly an hour a week during the build to review progress and answer questions. The exact commitment depends on your systems and the scope of the project.

Your input is particularly important when figures from different systems do not match. We can identify the differences, but your team helps us establish which figures reflect how the business actually operates.

Who will we actually be dealing with?

You'll work directly with the person building your solution, from the initial conversation through to delivery. That means clear communication, fewer handovers, and someone who understands the details of your project.

How do you work with our IT team or IT provider?

For the audit, a sample of your data is usually enough. For the build, we need read access to the relevant systems. We do not change your systems, install anything on production equipment, or need administrative rights by default. Your IT team or provider grants only the minimum access we need.
