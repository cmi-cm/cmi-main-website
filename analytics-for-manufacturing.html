This is for manufacturers who have outgrown spreadsheets

You're not short of data. You just don't have the capacity to analyse it. You have an ERP, a finance package, something running your manufacturing machinery, and someone is trying to build reports by hand using Excel while their backlog keeps growing.

That is fine at ten people, but at fifty or two hundred it starts costing real money, because decisions get delayed until everyone agrees on the correct figures in lengthy meetings, and by then the month has moved on.

We are not an ERP reseller and we are not going to tell you to replace any of your existing software. We connect what you already run, agree with your team once what each figure means, and build reports that everyone who needs access can see.

You will recognise this if

  • Month end depends on hours of manual reconciliation
  • Different departments see different figures
  • Nobody can say which products, services or contracts actually make money
  • Delivery performance isn't measured
  • Reports were built by someone who has since left

The main numbers manufacturers ask us for

Yours might differ, but the point of the first discovery call is to find out which numbers matter most to your business.

True margin by product and customer

Not price minus a standard cost, but the actual margin on every job, once materials, labour, scrap and rework are counted.

On time, in full delivery

Measured against the date you promised rather than a revised one, and broken down by customer and order line.

Scrap, waste and rework

What it costs, broken down by product, shift and machine, so you can tell whether a problem is in the process or just a one-off error.

Why manufacturing data is harder than it looks

Manufacturing reports need more than connected data. They need to reflect how your business actually operates, from production shifts to stock movements and customer orders.

A sales opportunity is not the same as a confirmed order. But ignoring your sales pipeline entirely can leave demand forecasts behind reality. Useful planning needs to account for likely orders, conversion rates and lead times.

A production day is not always a calendar day. When shifts cross midnight or production runs around the clock, grouping output by date alone can give you misleading figures. Reports need to follow the way your operation measures its working day.

A changed report can change the numbers people rely on. Without version history and controlled deployments, an accidental edit can go unnoticed and affect reporting downstream.

What we do about it

  • Sales opportunities and confirmed orders both used in planning, but kept separate
  • Reporting aligned to your production calendar and shift patterns
  • Version control and controlled deployments, so changes are tracked

Manufacturing analytics questions

The practical ones we get asked before anyone commits to anything.

Can reporting be limited so people only see their own site or department?

Yes. Access is set by role, so a site manager sees their own site or team, a sales manager sees their own accounts, and finance sees the full picture. Sensitive columns are hidden from anyone who doesn't need them.

Will the reports work on a phone or a tablet on the shop floor?

Yes, and they are specifically designed for it rather than being a resized desktop report.

How do you make sure the new numbers are right?

Every figure is checked against the reports you already trust before it goes live. Where a new figure differs from an old one, we show you exactly which records or calculations cause the difference and let you decide which is correct.
